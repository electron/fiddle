Principles and rules

Four ideas carry the whole system. When a case isn't covered here, decide it by these.

  • Glass for chrome, paper for workTitle bar, sidebar, status bar, menus and floating panes are translucent. Code, the console and anything you read at length sit on the solid sheet.
  • Cyan is the actionRun is a cyan capsule with a navy label, and cyan marks focus and selection. In light it flips to navy with a cyan label. Red means running or broken, nothing else.
  • Layers, not linesSurfaces separate by depth, blur and a lit edge. Hairlines are 0.5px and appear only where rows need them.
  • Chrome floats in capsulesToolbar controls gather into glass capsules. Everything inside the sheet uses continuous-corner rectangles.

Do

  • Take every value from a token. If a value is missing, add a token rather than a literal.
  • Keep one editor by default. The split is opt-in, from the tab row or ⌘\.
  • Use sentence case everywhere: buttons, menus, section heads, the status bar.
  • Keep corners concentric: an inner radius is the outer radius minus the inset.
  • Let the OS draw window corners, shadows and the window controls.

Don't

  • Put glass behind code, the console, dialogs' body copy or long text.
  • Use cyan for decoration, or red for anything that isn't running or broken.
  • Add gradients. The only ones are the primary sheen and the assist ring.
  • Use uppercase labels, borders between panes, or 1px dividers.
  • Stack a blurred overlay on another blurred surface without checking legibility.

Behaviour

One editor by defaultTabs for main.js, preload.js, renderer.js, index.html and styles.css. The process label (Main process, Preload, Renderer) sits at the right of the tab row, in ink-faint.
SplitThe columns button at the right end of the tab row, or ⌘\. Opens a second pane beside the current one showing renderer.js, or main.js when renderer.js is current. The tab row keeps driving the left pane. Press again to close. Tooltip: "Split editor ⌘\" or "Close split".
Pop-outEach pane header in split view carries a pop-out action. A popped-out file opens in its own window with the same chrome and sheet, and its tab shows a small window glyph until it's docked back.
SidebarGroups files by process: Main process, Preload, Renderer, then Packages with an "Add a package" field. Clicking a file opens its tab. The sidebar button in the title bar hides it; the sheet then runs to 8px from the left edge too.
ResizingDrag the sidebar edge (200 to 320px) and the top of the console (96px to half the sheet). Dividers are invisible until hover, then show a 2px cyan line.

Acceptance checklist

Check each item in dark and light, on macOS and Windows.

  • Tokens. No colour, radius, shadow or duration literal in component styles. Grep for # and rgba( outside the token file.
  • Material. The desktop shows through the title bar, sidebar and status bar; the sheet is fully opaque; the window corners and shadow are the OS's.
  • Fallback. With Reduce transparency on, and on Linux, the chrome is opaque and the app still looks finished.
  • Title bar. 56px, drag region works, traffic lights centred (macOS) or caption buttons clear (Windows), capsule centred in the window.
  • Default layout. One editor with five tabs, the process label at the right, console 160px, status bar 32px.
  • Split. The button and ⌘\ open and close a second pane; the tooltip text matches the state.
  • Run. All four states, fixed width, correct announcements, the Running pill in the status bar.
  • Errors. Tab badge, sidebar pill, error line, lens aligned to the code column, console row with a working link.
  • Menus. The version menu opens above the editor, sizes to its labels, and is readable over the chrome.
  • Corners. Squircles where supported, round elsewhere, concentric everywhere (menu 17 = item 12 + padding 5).
  • Hairlines. 0.5px, only between rows, above the console and under the tab row.
  • Type. Inter with optical sizes and the two features; Commit Mono in the editor and console; sentence case.
  • Focus. Visible on every control, never clipped by a parent's overflow.
  • Contrast. The table above holds; chrome text still passes over a black and a saturated wallpaper.
